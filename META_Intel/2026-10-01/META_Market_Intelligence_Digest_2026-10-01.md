## Executive headline

Two things moved today, and both are about compute supply.
- **HUMAIN raised its build target.** In an EnterpriseAM interview, CEO Tareq Amin put 2027 capacity at 1.7 GW. He also announced a separate, unsized "compute-infrastructure fund", next to a USD 2.5bn domestic data-centre fund managed by BSF Capital. No server, storage or cooling vendor has been named.
- **Micron confirmed the memory squeeze will get worse.** It reported FQ4 revenue of $54.2bn against a $50.0bn guide, and guided FQ1 at $61.5bn. It says supply in 2027–28 will be "much tighter" than in 2026, and more than 75% of 2027 output is already committed. It also expects double-digit unit declines in PCs and smartphones.
- **Competitors:** HPE booked the first AMD Helios rack order ($1.2bn, Vultr). That matters because AMD is HUMAIN's named partner for its joint AI-factory project.

**Lenovo META read:** HUMAIN is setting up a compute fund separate from its buildings fund, so the ISG question becomes who brings the rack and the financing. Lenovo needs a TruScale- or partner-financed AMD/NVIDIA rack offer in front of HUMAIN before HPE turns its Helios reference into a Saudi default. On IDG, Micron's guidance means 2027 device pricing will rise, not ease. Lock allocation now.
