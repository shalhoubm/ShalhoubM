**The Gulf data-centre economics sharpened.** Turner & Townsend ranks Saudi Arabia as the costliest Gulf market to build a data centre ($10.80/W, against $9.20/W in the UAE). Mubbadrah's CEO says its $3bn, 400 MW Salalah campus covers shell and power only: tenants bring their own servers, an estimated $7–9bn hardware pool with no OEM attached.
**Compute finance is splitting into haves and have-nots.** Anthropic filed for a Nasdaq listing ("ANT") and Meta launched a $35bn offering. Musk said debt markets have "completely dried up" for neoclouds, and the Nebius CEO conceded some capital sources are "temporarily closed". Nebius is HUMAIN/DataVolt's first named Oxagon tenant (OPP-105).
**Memory:** DRAMeXchange's Monday print is flat (DDR5 16Gb $61.333, +0.11%). The contracted pool keeps tightening: KB Securities says 70–80% of SK hynix's 2027 output is already under LTAs, and KeyBanc reports HP and Dell have extended Micron supply to 2031.
**Lenovo META read:** two of today's openings, the Salalah tenant hardware pool and the SDAIA DR expansion, are pure ISG infrastructure with no OEM named. The neocloud credit squeeze makes Lenovo's balance sheet and TruScale a selling point for Gulf AI-capacity buyers. Meanwhile Dell keeps collecting public reference wins in the region (Dangote; Cairo ICT title sponsor) while Lenovo has no META stage presence this week.

- **Summary:** In Turner & Townsend's Data Centre Construction Cost Index 2026–27, the Middle East averages $9.93/W, against a global high of $18.28/W in Tokyo. It also gives prefabricated, modular deployment a stronger argument. Use the index in ISG TCO conversations with HUMAIN, DataVolt, center3 and enterprise on-prem buyers.
