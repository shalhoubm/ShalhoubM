## Executive headline

The Gulf AI story has shifted from building capacity to making it survivable.
- **Rethink:** a day after AWS wrote off its Bahrain region and one UAE zone, Amazon disputed that the sites will never reopen but gave no rebuild commitment. AGBI reports that investors and hyperscalers now have to plan for deliberate multi-zone attacks.
- **Saudi demand:** in-Kingdom buyers are already acting. CNTXT and Tencent Cloud report war-driven multi-cloud demand, and Nomu-listed Edarat placed a SAR 127.5m order for GPU and cloud equipment with the OEM undisclosed.
- **Africa:** the US DFC put $155m into WIOCC's African cables and data centres, alongside Saudi-based Vision Invest, explicitly as an alternative to Huawei.

**Memory keeps tightening.**
- **Prices:** Seoul Economic Daily puts 64GB DDR5 server RDIMM contract prices at $1,500, 5.5 times a year ago.
- **Forecasts:** Citi sees supply tightness lasting until 2031.
- **Entry devices:** Omdia shows Africa's sub-$100 smartphone shipments down 34%.
- **Supply alternative:** Huawei pulled its Ascend roadmap forward and says demand exceeds supply.

**Lenovo META read:** Two things should happen this week.
- **Sell resilience:** turn AWS's damage into sovereign-resilience wins, starting with Edarat and CNTXT-type buyers.
- **Protect supply:** secure 2027 memory before server BOMs (bills of materials) reprice again. Goldman's new $1.5tn server-market model (per a secondary summary) shows Dell pulling ahead on AI-server share.
